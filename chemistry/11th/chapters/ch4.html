4.1 Chemical Bonds — An Overview

Atoms bond to achieve a stable electronic configuration (noble gas configuration). Three main types:

  • Ionic Bond: Transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal (electrovalent bond). Example: NaCl
  • Covalent Bond: Sharing of electron pairs between atoms. Example: H₂, Cl₂
  • Coordinate Bond: Shared pair donated by one atom only (dative bond). Example: NH₄⁺, BF₃·NH₃

4.2 Ionic Bond

Formed by complete transfer of electrons from one atom to another. Factors favouring ionic bond:

  • Low ionization enthalpy of the metal
  • High electron gain enthalpy of the non-metal
  • High lattice enthalpy

4.3 Covalent Bond — Lewis Structures

Lewis dot structures represent bonding and lone pairs. Steps: (1) Count total valence electrons, (2) Identify central atom (least electronegative), (3) Connect atoms with single bonds, (4) Distribute remaining electrons as lone pairs, (5) Form multiple bonds if octet is incomplete.

Formal Charge

FC = Valence electrons − Lone pair electrons − ½(Bonding electrons)

The best Lewis structure has the smallest formal charges on each atom.

Limitations of Octet Rule

  • Incomplete octet: BF₃ (B has 6 e⁻), BeCl₂ (Be has 4 e⁻)
  • Expanded octet: PCl₅, SF₆ (elements in period 3+ use d-orbitals)
  • Odd-electron molecules: NO, NO₂

4.4 VSEPR Theory

4.5 Hybridization

Intermixing of atomic orbitals of similar energies to form new equivalent hybrid orbitals:

  • sp: 2 hybrid orbitals, linear, 180°. Example: BeCl₂, C₂H₂
  • sp²: 3 hybrid orbitals, trigonal planar, 120°. Example: BF₃, C₂H₄
  • sp³: 4 hybrid orbitals, tetrahedral, 109.5°. Example: CH₄, NH₃, H₂O
  • sp³d: 5 hybrid orbitals, trigonal bipyramidal. Example: PCl₅
  • sp³d²: 6 hybrid orbitals, octahedral. Example: SF₆

4.6 Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT)

Proposed by Hund and Mulliken. Atomic orbitals combine to form molecular orbitals that belong to the molecule as a whole.

  • Bonding MOs: lower energy, formed by constructive interference
  • Anti-bonding MOs (σ*, π*): higher energy, formed by destructive interference

Bond Order = ½(Nb − Na) where Nb = bonding electrons, Na = anti-bonding electrons

If Bond Order > 0, molecule is stable. Higher bond order = stronger, shorter bond.

MO Energy Order

For O₂, F₂: σ1s < σ*1s < σ2s < σ*2s < σ2pz < (π2px = π2py) < (π*2px = π*2py) < σ*2pz

For B₂, C₂, N₂: σ1s < σ*1s < σ2s < σ*2s < (π2px = π2py) < σ2pz < (π*2px = π*2py) < σ*2pz

4.7 Hydrogen Bonding

Electrostatic attraction between H bonded to a highly electronegative atom (F, O, N) and a lone pair on another electronegative atom.

  • Intermolecular: Between different molecules (e.g., HF, H₂O)
  • Intramolecular: Within the same molecule (e.g., o-nitrophenol)

Don't confuse hybridization with shape. sp³ hybridization gives tetrahedral ARRANGEMENT, but the molecular SHAPE depends on lone pairs: sp³ with 1 LP = trigonal pyramidal, sp³ with 2 LP = bent.
The MO energy order is different for B₂, C₂, N₂ (σ2p after π2p) vs O₂, F₂ (σ2p before π2p). This is due to mixing of 2s and 2p orbitals in lighter elements.
O₂ is paramagnetic — this cannot be explained by Lewis structures or VBT. Only MOT correctly predicts this.

Chapter Summary

  • Ionic bond: electron transfer; Covalent bond: electron sharing; Coordinate bond: one-sided donation
  • Lewis structures show bonding and lone pairs; Formal charge minimization gives best structure
  • VSEPR predicts shape from electron pair geometry around central atom
  • Hybridization: sp (linear), sp² (trigonal planar), sp³ (tetrahedral), sp³d (TBP), sp³d² (octahedral)
  • MOT: Bond Order = ½(Nb − Na); O₂ is paramagnetic (2 unpaired e⁻ in π*)
  • Hydrogen bonding: H—F, H—O, H—N interactions; affects boiling points significantly
