3.1 Historical Development

Döbereiner's Triads (1829): Groups of three elements with similar properties where the atomic mass of the middle element was approximately the average of the other two. Example: Li, Na, K

Newlands' Law of Octaves (1866): When elements are arranged by increasing atomic mass, every 8th element has similar properties (like musical notes). Worked only up to calcium.

Mendeleev's Periodic Law (1869): "Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic masses." He arranged 63 elements in a table with 8 groups and left gaps for undiscovered elements (Eka-boron = Sc, Eka-aluminium = Ga, Eka-silicon = Ge).

3.2 Modern Periodic Law

Moseley (1913): "Properties of elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers."

Structure of Modern Periodic Table

  • 7 Periods (horizontal rows): Period 1 (2 elements), Period 2-3 (8 each), Period 4-5 (18 each), Period 6 (32), Period 7 (32)
  • 18 Groups (vertical columns): Numbered 1 to 18 (IUPAC)
  • 4 Blocks: s-block (Groups 1-2), p-block (Groups 13-18), d-block (Groups 3-12), f-block (lanthanoids + actinoids)

3.3 Periodic Trends

Atomic Radius

Decreases across a period (left to right) due to increasing nuclear charge.

Increases down a group due to addition of new shells.

Ionization Enthalpy (IE)

Energy required to remove the outermost electron from an isolated gaseous atom.

Increases across a period (higher nuclear charge holds electrons tighter).

Decreases down a group (outermost electron is farther from nucleus).

Exceptions: IE of Be > B (2s² more stable than 2s²2p¹), IE of N > O (half-filled p³ more stable than p⁴)

Electron Gain Enthalpy (EGE)

Energy released when an electron is added to a neutral gaseous atom.

Becomes more negative across a period (atoms accept electrons more readily).

Becomes less negative down a group (atom size increases).

Exception: EGE of F is less negative than Cl (small size of F causes electron-electron repulsion)

Electronegativity

Tendency of an atom to attract shared pair of electrons in a chemical bond (Pauling scale).

Increases across a period, decreases down a group. Fluorine is the most electronegative element (4.0).

Metallic/Non-metallic Character

Metallic character decreases across a period and increases down a group.

Oxide Nature

Oxides change from basic → amphoteric → acidic across a period.

Chapter Summary

  • Modern periodic law: Properties are periodic function of atomic number
  • 7 periods, 18 groups, 4 blocks (s, p, d, f)
  • Atomic radius: ↓ across period, ↑ down group
  • IE: ↑ across period, ↓ down group (exceptions: Be>B, N>O)
  • EGE: more negative across period (exception: Cl > F)
  • Electronegativity: ↑ across period, ↓ down group (F is highest)
  • Oxides: basic → amphoteric → acidic across a period
