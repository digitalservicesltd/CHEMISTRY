  • Understand thermodynamic terms: system, surroundings, state functions
  • Study First Law of Thermodynamics and internal energy
  • Learn about enthalpy (ΔH), enthalpy of reaction, formation, combustion
  • Understand Hess's Law and Born-Haber cycle
  • Study Second Law — entropy (ΔS)
  • Learn Gibbs free energy and spontaneity

5.1 Thermodynamic Terms

System: Part of the universe under study. Surroundings: Everything else.

Types of systems:

  • Open system: Exchanges both energy and matter (e.g., open beaker)
  • Closed system: Exchanges only energy, not matter (e.g., sealed flask)
  • Isolated system: No exchange of energy or matter (e.g., thermos flask)

State functions: Depend only on the state, not the path (e.g., U, H, S, G, P, V, T)

Path functions: Depend on the path (e.g., work W, heat q)

5.2 First Law of Thermodynamics

ΔU = q + W (Energy can neither be created nor destroyed)

Sign convention: q positive = heat absorbed by system; W positive = work done on system

For expansion against external pressure: W = −P_ext × ΔV

At constant volume: ΔU = q_v (heat at constant volume)

At constant pressure: ΔH = q_p (heat at constant pressure)

5.3 Enthalpy (H)

H = U + PV and ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT

where Δng = moles of gaseous products − moles of gaseous reactants

Types of Enthalpy Changes

  • Standard enthalpy of formation (ΔfH°): Enthalpy change when 1 mole of compound is formed from elements in standard state
  • Standard enthalpy of combustion (ΔcH°): Enthalpy change when 1 mole burns completely in O₂
  • Enthalpy of atomization: Energy to convert 1 mole into gaseous atoms
  • Lattice enthalpy: Energy to completely separate 1 mole of ionic compound into gaseous ions
  • Bond enthalpy: Average energy to break 1 mole of a particular bond in gaseous state

5.4 Hess's Law

The total enthalpy change in a reaction is the same regardless of the pathway — enthalpy is a state function.

ΔrH° = Σ ΔfH°(products) − Σ ΔfH°(reactants)

5.5 Second Law — Entropy

Entropy (S): Measure of disorder or randomness. For a spontaneous process in an isolated system, entropy always increases.

ΔS = q_rev / T

ΔS_total = ΔS_system + ΔS_surroundings > 0 (for spontaneous process)

5.6 Gibbs Free Energy

G = H − TS and ΔG = ΔH − TΔS

  • ΔG < 0 → Spontaneous (exergonic)
  • ΔG > 0 → Non-spontaneous (endergonic)
  • ΔG = 0 → Equilibrium

ΔG° = −RT ln K (relation to equilibrium constant)

Q1. For a reaction to be spontaneous at all temperatures, the conditions are:
ΔH > 0, ΔS < 0
ΔH < 0, ΔS > 0
ΔH > 0, ΔS > 0
ΔH < 0, ΔS < 0
When ΔH < 0 (exothermic) and ΔS > 0 (entropy increases), ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is always negative, making the reaction spontaneous at all temperatures.

Sign convention: W = −PΔV (IUPAC), but some older textbooks use W = +PΔV. Always check which convention is being used.
In ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT, students forget that Δng counts only gaseous moles (not solids or liquids).
ΔG° = −RT ln K uses R = 8.314 J/mol·K and T in Kelvin. Ensure consistent units — if ΔH is in kJ, convert before using with TΔS in J.

Chapter Summary

  • First Law: ΔU = q + W; energy is conserved
  • Enthalpy: H = U + PV; at constant P, ΔH = qp
  • ΔH = ΔU + ΔngRT connects enthalpy and internal energy
  • Hess's Law: ΔH is path-independent (state function)
  • Entropy measures disorder; always increases for spontaneous process in isolated system
  • Gibbs energy: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS; ΔG < 0 = spontaneous
  • At equilibrium: ΔG = 0, ΔG° = −RT ln K
